## 1. Thesis Statement

Muslim technical talent in India is abundant, yet professionally fragmented. Brilliant engineers and students are scattered across cities, institutions, and companies — often without access to the mentorship, referrals, and networks that convert raw ability into opportunity.

The **Indian Muslim Tech Network (IMTN)** is an open-source initiative designed to address this fragmentation. Our aim is to establish a **unified, high-signal, and strictly vetted ecosystem** that its members can trust.

Through collaborative infrastructure, IMTN seeks to drive **systemic upward mobility** — closing the gap between localised academic talent and global industry opportunity. We do this through three mechanisms:

- **Structured mentorship** from experienced professionals,
- A **decentralised job referral engine**, and
- **Peer-led technical upskilling**.

We regard this work as more than a professional network. Knowledge in our tradition is a trust (*amānah*), and sharing it is an act of ongoing charity (*ṣadaqah jāriyah*). When one of us rises and pulls another up, the entire community is strengthened.

## 2. Core Goals

To realise this thesis, the project is organised into actionable, community-driven objectives across three horizons.

### 2.1 Short-Term Goals (Infrastructure and Foundation)

- **Establish automated open-source governance.** Build a secure, transparent repository setup in which contributors can propose community guidelines, Code of Conduct updates, and technology-stack decisions through GitHub Pull Requests — governance by consultation (*shūrā*), in the open.
- **Build the secure gatekeeper system.** Develop a verification workflow (using tools such as Discord bots, automated LinkedIn validation, or GitHub OAuth) to ensure a safe, troll-free environment before members unlock the main community channels.
- **Launch the minimum viable funnel.** Deploy a fast, accessible landing page (via Vercel) and a central directory to capture early expressions of interest from senior engineers and university students alike.

### 2.2 Mid-Term Goals (Community Engagement and Value Loops)

- **Deploy a decentralised referral pipeline.** Create a structured repository or internal platform where senior developers can share internal openings and members can safely request referrals based on verified skill sets.
- **Launch the peer-review and interview engine.** Establish recurring, community-led technical cycles — including weekly Data Structures and Algorithms (DSA) mock interviews, system design teardowns, and résumé review clinics.
- **Onboard 100+ high-signal mentors.** Actively source and invite senior engineers, tech leads, and product managers from prominent Indian startups, FAANG, and global remote companies to anchor the community's advisory layer — embodying the principle that *"the best of people are those most beneficial to people."*

### 2.3 Long-Term Goals (Sustenance and Ecosystem Impact)

- **Drive open-source ecosystem contributions.** Incubate community-driven open-source projects under the IMTN GitHub organisation, giving students and freshers real-world codebase experience that strengthens their résumés.
- **Bridge the university-to-industry gap.** Form organic partnerships with student tech leads, GDSC (Google Developer Student Clubs) chapters, and placement cells at major Indian universities with significant Muslim student populations.
- **Cultivate halal tech leadership.** Provide a space for discussions on navigating corporate ethics, exploring compliant startup opportunities, and fostering executive leadership among minority professionals across the Indian subcontinent — so that professional success and principled conduct advance together.
